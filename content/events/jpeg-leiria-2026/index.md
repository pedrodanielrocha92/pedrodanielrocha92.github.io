Attending the IEEE SPS / JPEG workshop on AI-based media representation left me with several ideas about the future of visual information.

### From Compression to Understanding

Two research communities are becoming increasingly connected: multimedia coding and pattern recognition. Traditionally, one has focused on representing information efficiently for humans, while the other has focused on extracting meaning from visual data. Both are ultimately addressing variations of the same fundamental problem: how to build efficient and meaningful representations of visual data.

This convergence is becoming increasingly evident as learning-based compression, coding for machines, and foundation models blur the boundaries between representation, compression, and understanding. The representation itself is no longer just a means of storing or transmitting information; it is becoming the central object that enables both efficient communication and machine intelligence.

### Trustworthy Digital Content

The emerging JPEG Trust framework is a particularly important development. While JPEG has long been associated with image compression, it is now extending its role toward standardizing mechanisms for media provenance, authenticity, integrity, attribution, and rights management. As synthetic media is generated at scale, interoperability around trust may become just as important as interoperability around compression.

Trust has always been a fundamental value of society. What is changing in the age of generative AI is not the importance of trust, but how difficult it is to establish in digital environments. Preserving that trust will require technology, standards, legislation, and education working together. Standards provide the common infrastructure that allows trust to scale across systems and organizations.

### Reflections

Despite these challenges, I remain optimistic. Every transformative technology raises legitimate concerns, but society adapts by developing new tools, standards, and institutions. AI is no different. Our challenge is to build systems that are not only more capable, but also more trustworthy.
